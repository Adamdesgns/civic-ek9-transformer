# Civic Type R (EK9) character: car body

Authored procedurally in Blender (`blender/civic-ek9_build/`, package `ek9`). This stage is the car only: no robot, rig, transformation or game export yet. Build, review renders and a scrubbable save:

```bash
blender -b --factory-startup --python blender/civic-ek9_build/review.py -- <tag> [view ...]
```

Views are the perspective cameras in `ek9/build.py` (`heroF`, `heroR`, `heroS`, `nose`, `tail`, ..., and `photo`, fitted to the reference photo) and orthographic `o_side`, `o_front`, `o_rear`, `o_top`. The file is saved as `blender/civic-ek9-car.blend`; renders go to `blender/renders/civic-ek9/`.

## Reference and datums

- The car is one real car: TopRank stock #111040, a 1999 GF-EK9 (facelift), photographed from every side in `refs/ref_images/ek9-gallery/yellow-111040_*` (gitignored; the angle table is in the Projects `docs/handoffs/2026-09-26-ek9-reference-gallery.md`). It replaced the earlier lowered look (17 in black six-spokes, black splitter), which is backed up as `blender/civic-ek9-car-lowered.blend`.
- Photo-matching: a pinhole camera fitted to landmarks with known model positions, and a render through it overlaid on the photo. In the side profiles every landmark sits near one plane (the flank), so the camera's distance is not determined, and centreline silhouettes (nose, tail) carry a few cm of uncertainty. Compare features at the same depth against each other (the bumper face against the hood just above it), not absolute end positions. The official length (4,185 mm) puts the camera about 15 m off.
- Outlines come from `refs/ref_images/civic-ek-hatch.png` (gitignored). `tools/civic-ek9/measure_blueprint.py` measures the silhouettes, and `grid_crop.py` / `grid_front.py` read the internal lines.
- Blueprint to design mapping: `x = 0.9855 x_bp` (1.695 m width), `z = 1.0144 z_bp - 0.093` (1.325 m roof, 0.13 m sills), `f` unchanged. Keep new datums in blueprint proportion.
- `tools/civic-ek9/overlay.py` draws the blueprint over an ortho render. Check any shape change against it.
- **The face follows the reference photo, not the blueprint.** The blueprint is a base-model EK; the car has the facelift lamps and a Type R nose. Use the blueprint there for positions and overall size only. So the lamp tip sits lower and further inboard (x 0.365) than the blueprint's (0.40), and the grille is a slot rather than the blueprint's full-height surround.

## Shape: envelope ∩ fascias

- **Envelope:** constant-f slices describe hoods, roofs and flanks well, and forward-facing faces badly: rings twist and crease where a surface turns to face forward. So the envelope (a section loft, `body.section_ctrl`) is carried past both ends of the car (`NOSE_EXT`, `TAIL_EXT`) at its own shape.
- **Fascias:** the front and rear fascias are stacks of horizontal waterlines, the plan outline inset by a height-dependent face profile. They carve the ends.
  - The hood's leading edge, the lamp tops and the hatch glass's lower edge are simply where the fascias meet the envelope.
  - Those meeting edges are rolled by relaxing a band either side of them (`body._round_seams`, `SEAM_BAND` 25 mm). Faces are tagged by source (`src`: envelope, front, rear) before the booleans; the MANIFOLD solver keeps the attribute, and the seam is where the tag changes.
  - A bevel cannot round these edges: the fascia side is 3 mm waterline strips and the boolean leaves slivers, so a clamped bevel stops at a few mm (the edge stays a torn stair-step, with angle or weight limits alike) and an unclamped one folds over. The relaxation triangulates the band first (a relaxed n-gon bends) and needs vertices on both sides, hence envelope stations at most `END_STEP` (5 mm) apart through `END_ZONES`.
  - Every inset gets the same band, so a skin's inner surface rolls at least as much as its outer one and the shell never thins at the edge.
  - The relaxation is a Laplacian smooth, which shrinks a convex surface. Where a fascia takes over from a flank at a few degrees on a convex corner, it pressed a groove a few mm deep down the band. So round the rear corners (`CORNER_ZONE`) the fade starts at 9 degrees (`ROUND_FADE_CORNER`), and only real creases get rolled there.
  - The front bumper's underside edge (fascia meeting the flat envelope floor, `body.ROLL_ZONE`) is relaxed on a finer, quad-dominant band: its edges are split to 6 mm or less and only n-gons are triangulated. The floor's section spans are about 14 cm wide, and a fully triangulated band there pulled the roll into a sawtooth that read as a crumpled, torn edge in paint. The rest of the car keeps the plain triangulated band. Where a fascia corner seam meets the floor (the bumper's lower corners) a small nub remains.
- **Flanks:** they follow the plan outline up to the lamps (the `_W` track copies `PLAN_F` / `PLAN_R`). The lateral blend (`w`) only starts once the plan turns about 17°, so the fascias cut the corners and not the flanks.
  - Cutting a flank at a shallow angle leaves a near-tangent seam, and splitting skins by distance from the fascia fails there.
- **Plan outlines** are splined through a mirrored phantom point past the tip, so every waterline crosses x = 0 square (a one-sided spline left a V, a fold down the hatch and bumpers). Corners are drawn as arcs with a point every 8 degrees or so; one knot carrying a large turn prints a groove through every waterline.
- **Insets** must stay below the local plan radius or waterlines swallowtail. Each plan sample scales its whole height profile by one factor, `0.75 R / d_max` over the waterlines the body shows (below `zt`); clamping each waterline separately switched on and off from ring to ring and made the lower corners lumpy. Waterlines above the body only get a soft clamp.
- **Greenhouse:** the side glass stops `car.WIN_DROP` (4.5 cm) under the blueprint's glass line (1.268), and along the roof the roof edge rolls at 3 cm instead of 4.5 cm (`body.ROOF_EDGE_R`).
  - At the blueprint's line the glass ran up into the roof edge's rounding (zr about 1.27). From the low three-quarter cameras the roof side above it foreshortened to nothing, where the reference photo shows a band of body-colour roof over the side glass, and the greenhouse read as a tall box.
  - The door window keeps the A-pillar edge's line. The quarter window's drop fades out below z 1.05, so its rear sweep and tip keep the blueprint's lines.
  - The yellow rail above the windows is continuous. The A-pillar and quarter regions are joined above the glass before the shell is cut; the black B-pillar garnish stops at the sash top.
- **Flank curvature:** `_TUMBLE` leans the upper doors and quarters in about 6 degrees (2.4-2.7 cm), and the sill corner tucks 3.2 cm (`body.SILL_TUCK`), so the sides curve (sky above, ground below in the paint) instead of reading as a flat slab. The ends keep the lamps' original flank.
- **Shoulder crease:** the character-line key has a 7 mm radius and two samples in each span beside it (`ALLOC_HALF`), so the flank turns from 4° to 21° within about 1.2 cm and the highlight breaks along one line from the front fender to the tail lamps. Don't duplicate the key (`bspline_closed` needs one allocation per control point) or raise `_LEDGE` (it moves the glass base and every glass cutter).
- **Skins** are `shape(0) - shape(t)`, with sections offset through their control polygons and waterlines through their insets. This gives an exact shell at concave and convex edges. Solidify would self-intersect at the bumper step.
- **Rear face** (`FACE_R`, the blueprint's side silhouette): tuck-under, a vertical bumper face, a tight shoulder at 0.52-0.56 and the lamp / hatch-panel plane 8 cm behind the tip, with no ledge. Two corrections ride on it in `_fascia_field`:
  - At the corners `FACE_R_CORNER` blends in by `(x / REAR_CORNER_X)^4`: the lamp level stands only 2 cm inside the bumper's plan there, so the lamps wrap a square corner and run 16 cm down the flank (the blueprint's corner line at f -1.954).
  - Between the shoulder and z 0.80 the face is flattened across the plate (90 % of the plan's recession taken out inside |x| 0.30, fading out by 0.55), as the blueprint's top view has it; otherwise the plate recess rim set the side silhouette 1.1 cm short. The flattening fades out again from 0.80 to 0.93: the blueprint's glass lower edge curves forward like the bumper, and a flattened rake dipped the glass edge 1.6 cm at |x| 0.35.
  - Above 0.90 the face runs 2-8 mm under the hatch glass and meets it at about z 0.945, 2.5 cm above the blueprint's glass edge. Moving that seam to 0.92 would pull the 25 mm seam roll into the tail-lamp tops (0.90), which is what tore them before.
  - **Rear corners:** the fascia's waterlines start `margin` (8 mm) outside the flank at the seam station. Behind the arch, `_W` eases out from the plan by 13 mm at f -1.94 and then turns with the corner arc, staying outside. The fascia therefore crosses the flank on that ramp: at about 3-4 degrees, near f -1.84, and at the same station at every height from the tuck-under to the lamp tops. It then carries the whole corner, as the flank's own section swept round the plan arc, blending into the face profile as the plan turns.
    - A flank that ran straight on (0.765 at f -2.0) turned in, back out, and was then cut by the turning fascia at 20-30 degrees. The seam roll left a crumpled vertical fold down both rear bumper corners.
    - `_W` there must stay outside the fascia at every height. Check a change by tabulating the fascia waterline against `side_profile` round the corner: the gap should cross zero once, gently, and then keep growing.

## Panels

- **Skin split:** each skin is split once (`car.Skins`): `env` lies away from the fascia faces, `fF` within 8 mm of the front one.
  - The nose (grille surround) comes from the fascia part, and the hatch panel is the solid within 8 mm of the rear face (see Rear). Hood, fenders and glass come from the rest, so their shared seams follow the carved edge exactly.
  - Splitting once, instead of re-cutting per part, keeps the build under a minute.
- **Headlamps:** `parts.LAMP_REAR_PLAN` defines one smooth rear edge in plan, shared by the lens, housing and hood/fender pockets. The side-view cutter only rounds the outer return. Two competing front/side elevation cuts previously pinched the hood around the lamp.
  - The lens is the outer surface inside the window, with a 1.5 mm clear shell and black gasket. The housing remains one closed solid. Two broad elliptical reflector bowls replace the small circular projector-like bowls; their rims follow the curved lens with 12 mm clearance.
  - The hood's lower limit follows the bumper top. Its headlamp edge comes entirely from the shared lamp pocket, rather than a second front-elevation cut.
- **Grille:** the dark grille slot (`GRILLE_SLOT`) is cut through the hood's front face. It is wider at the top and about a third of the lamp height. Behind the slot, the `grilleMesh` recess has black walls from 3 mm in and a floor 30 mm in.
  - The floor has its own slot, `grilleDark`, which the game side needs as well: dielectric black, roughness 0.8, specular level 0.2, since the open area reflects nothing. A plain plastic or metallic floor caught the low sun's sheen and read mid-grey from a front three-quarter camera.
  - A black diamond mesh (`parts.lattice`, `meshDark`, 7 mm pitch) spans the slot 12 mm behind the nose skin, and the badge seats on it. Without it the slot read as a painted black stripe.
- **Hood:** the grille remains part of the hood. `hood_front_prism` sets only its lower edge; the shared headlamp pockets define the smooth edge over the lamps. `dims.HOOD_SEAM` still runs straight from the cowl along the fender top and meets the lamp about 10 cm inboard of its outer corner.

- **Tail lamps** (`parts.tail_lamp_cutter`): a rear-view outline, outboard of an inner edge that leans in toward the bottom (`tail_lamp_x`, 0.600 to 0.568), intersected with a side-view rectangle from f -1.793 back, z 0.60 to `TAIL_LAMP_TOP` 0.90, its lower front corner rounded.   - The housing (`parts.tail_housing`, `tailReflector`) copies the headlamp's: the deep skin from 12 mm in under the lamp grown 3 mm (`trim`), minus a cavity 30 mm deep inside the lamp shrunk 4 mm (`tailReflector` slot). ## Blender pitfalls met here

- **Hidden objects:** a viewport-hidden object is not evaluated, so booleans on its copies silently do nothing. Hide temporary skins from renders only.
- **Boolean materials:** a boolean hands the cutter's material slot and flat shading to the new faces. `shape_object` resets both.
  - The first boolean also appends the cutter's slot (`interior`) to the object, and in index mode every later cutter's faces then land on `interior`: the plate recess floor rendered black. A pocket that must show the panel's own slot has to carry that slot itself and go in with `TRANSFER` (`B.cut_shell` does this).
- **Boolean inputs:** the MANIFOLD solver needs closed solids, one mesh without overlapping shells. A partial-arc `lathe` is open at its arc ends (cap them), and parts that overlap (liner band and wall, the two mesh strip sets) are booleaned separately or unioned first. Hand-built sweeps and lathes can come out inside out: pass them through `kit.cutter_obj` (it fixes normals) before a boolean. An inverted wiper arm and boss unioned into doubled vertices, which `kit.clean` then welded into non-manifold edges.
- **Bevel NaNs:** the bevel modifier emits NaN vertices on some boolean slivers, which fan out below the car. `kit.clean` (weld at 0.02 mm, dissolve degenerate faces) before `kit.finish` removes them on every current part. `bevel_is_sane` stays as a guard and prints "bevel dropped".
  - It can also emit uninitialised coordinates (x about -2.5e11, y and z near 0). They drew thin black lines across the sky and stretched the sun's shadow bounds until the mirror and bumper shadows vanished, so the guard also rejects any vertex outside the part's own bounds.
  - The trigger was a union of two solids that share a face on the same inset surface (the pillar strip and the ring both on `rear12`, the fin's ends and the lip both on `rear6`). Build such regions from one cutter (`B.bool_mesh` union of the cutters) cut once, or keep the parts apart.
- **Sharp edges after a boolean:** a part cut against the body (spoiler, mirror sail) keeps smooth shading across its hard contact edge, and the averaged normals shaded its thin faces in dark teeth. `Mesh.set_sharp_from_angle(30°)` after the boolean splits them.
- **Shadow serration:** at close range EEVEE's sun shadow map prints a sawtooth along the shadow of small proud parts (the mouldings). The geometry's contact line is straight; turn the sun's shadow off to tell the two apart.
- **Slivers move with the body:** a shape change anywhere reshuffles how the booleans slice the whole-body skins, so a panel far from the change can gain micron-thin triangles (the zero-area check catches them). Weld that panel harder (`panel(..., weld=1e-4)`) instead of loosening the check.
- **Default scene:** `--factory-startup` leaves the default cube at the origin. `build.clear_defaults` removes it.
- **Vertex groups** live on the mesh, so after a modifier is applied (`kit.apply_modifiers` swaps the mesh) look a group up again by name.
- **BVH trees from boolean meshes:** `BVHTree.FromPolygons` fans the concave n-gons a boolean leaves into wrong triangles, so rays pass through holes. Build trees from `loop_triangles` (`parts.tri_tree`).
- **On-surface tests:** judge whether a face lies on a surface by its vertices, not its centre. The waterline n-gons are long and curved, and their centres lie off the surface.
- **Chrome in a cavity:** random per-cell normal tilts (Voronoi) read as crushed glass close up and as white confetti from the hero cameras, and in the 5 cm reverse band as grit. The reflector slots now use structured bands instead (`mats.REFLECTOR_BANDS`): a fixed tilt per horizontal band of object z. The headlamp housing uses 9 mm bands at tilt 0.035 over aluminised metal (roughness 0.18), and the tail reflector 6 mm flutes. The cups stay smooth (`lampCup`).
- **Clear lens:** EEVEE's screen-space refraction fell back to the sky and turned the lens milky. The lens is a black-based BSDF (Fresnel reflection only) added to a transparent BSDF, alpha-blended.

## Staging

- The world is Poly Haven's `rural_asphalt_road` HDRI (CC0, `refs/hdri/rural_asphalt_road_2k.hdr`, gitignored). Without it the stage falls back to a Nishita sky over plain asphalt.
- Its values are measured from the file, with the disc clamped at 8: the sun at elevation 45.4 degrees, 36.1 degrees off the image-centre road; the sun and clamped-sky irradiance per channel (`scene.HDRI_SUN_E`, `HDRI_SKY_E`, about 4 : 1).
- The mapping puts the image road ahead of the nose and the HDRI's sun 36.1 degrees off the nose on the key side (`scene.key_side`): mirror y and rotate z -90 degrees for the left, rotate z +90 degrees for the right.
- The world is clamped so the disc leaves the world probe, and EEVEE's own sun extraction is off. `ek.sun` carries the disc's energy with crisp shadows at elevation 28 degrees: lower than the capture's sun, so the flanks the hero cameras see are lit, as in the photo's low sun.
- White balance (`HDRI_WB`) scales the world and the sun so lit asphalt reads near-neutral grey (the capture is warm). A warm light turned Sunlight Yellow toward orange.
- The ground is the HDRI projected from its capture height (1.6 m) onto a 1 km plane, divided per channel by the capture's own sun + sky irradiance. That gives the road's albedo (about 0.09), which the stage re-lights, the car's shadow included. The projected road meets the background at the horizon.
- The `photo` view is the camera fitted to the reference photo's landmarks (the car's right front, 18.5 mm, 1422 x 930). The key swaps to the right side for it, since the photo shows the right flank in low sun.
- The view transform is Khronos PBR Neutral (the operator behind `THREE.NeutralToneMapping`). AgX washed Sunlight Yellow out to cream.
- Ortho renders hide the ground.

## Checks

`blender -b blender/civic-ek9-car.blend --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/civic-ek9/check_model.py [-- mesh|holes]` checks two things (the hole rays run at design z plus the scene's `ek_ride`):

- **Mesh:** zero-area faces stay under 1 % per object, and the layered parts and the parts cut against the body (spoiler, mirror sails) are one piece each.
- **Holes:** a 1 cm ray grid covers both sides, the front and the rear. A ray counts as a hole when its first hit is the floor, a liner, an `interior` face, or the windshield from behind or from deep inside the side outline. It also counts when a ray inside the side silhouette hits nothing. Wheel arches are exempt, and a hole needs all four neighbours 8 mm away to fail too, so shut lines pass.
